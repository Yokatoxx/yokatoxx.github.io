This project was built during a 48-hour game jam centered on the theme "two in one." As the team's primary programmer, I was responsible for turning our design decisions into working systems under intense time pressure. From the first hour, it was clear that scope discipline would determine whether we finished or not.


Our first design session focused on finding a mechanic that expressed the theme in a way that was both honest and achievable. We discussed split-screen co-op and dual-character control before settling on dimension-switching. The concept was clean: the player moves through a world inhabited by ghosts from two separate dimensions, and must switch into a ghost's native dimension to attack it. This interpretation mapped directly to the theme, required no additional art pipelines, and could be prototyped quickly. I began with the switch toggle and the vulnerability logic, validating the core feel before writing a single line of upgrade code.

Once the combat loop worked, we identified replayability as the main weak point. Rather than designing more levels or enemies, we added a soul economy: players collect souls from defeated ghosts and spend them on character upgrades between rounds. This roguelite-adjacent decision gave players meaningful choices without demanding extra assets or design time from the team. The structure also aligned naturally with the Die & Retry loop we were building toward.

The primary challenge was psychological as much as technical. Every emerging idea had to pass a quick cost-benefit check: does this improve the experience enough to justify the time it costs? Most features were cut or deferred. Holding that line consistently was one of the most valuable things I practiced during the jam. On the technical side, the dimension-switch state required careful management to avoid audio and visual glitches on transition, which pushed me to write cleaner, more modular code than I might have under less pressure. Overall, this jam sharpened my instinct for prioritization and confirmed that a focused mechanic executed well outperforms an ambitious one left unfinished.
