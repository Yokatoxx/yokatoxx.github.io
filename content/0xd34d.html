Design Challenge

0xD34D was created during the Epic Games Game Jam 2025 around the theme “Here we go again.” Our interpretation focused on the die-and-retry loop: short, intense encounters where failure should lead to a better decision rather than a long interruption.

As Level Designer, I worked on player flow, enemy placement and encounter readability for a fast-paced top-down shooter. My main objective was to make every death feel understandable and useful to the player.

Level Design Principles

01 · Readability

The player must understand the immediate threat before committing to a movement or attack.

02 · Pressure

Tight spaces, limited sightlines and enemy density create urgency without relying on long encounters.

03 · Feedback

A death should reveal something about the room, the enemy placement or the player’s positioning.

04 · Mastery

Repeated attempts let the player turn room knowledge into faster and more confident decisions.

From Research to Blockout

I used Hotline Miami as a reference for its room structure, limited sightlines and fast decision-making. I did not copy its aesthetics; I extracted spatial principles that could support our own gameplay loop.

Designing for an Isometric Camera

Moving from paper layouts to Unreal Engine revealed a specific constraint: the isometric camera changed what the player could see. Enemy positions that looked correct in a standard top-down layout could become difficult to spot from the actual gameplay perspective.

I therefore prioritised threat visibility over purely tactical placement. Angles, positions and encounter composition had to be reconsidered from the player’s real point of view.

What I Learned

This project reinforced how strongly camera perspective shapes level design. A layout cannot be evaluated independently from the way the player sees and reads it.

It also confirmed the value of early paper prototyping: testing flow and enemy placement before touching the engine made it possible to explore ideas quickly and focus in-engine time on validating the most promising layouts.

Key takeaway
Fast-paced difficulty only feels fair when the player can understand the space, identify the cause of failure and try again immediately.
