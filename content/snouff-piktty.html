For this project, I took on the role of lead programmer for a one-week mobile game jam developed with my team. From the start, we aligned on a clear principle: keep the concept simple, but make it feel genuinely mobile-native. Rather than porting a standard mechanic to touchscreen, we decided to build the entire experience around the accelerometer, which pushed me into territory I had never explored before.


My first challenge was learning how to work with Unity's mobile input APIs. I had never programmed for mobile before, so understanding how to read accelerometer data, normalize it across devices with different sensitivities, and translate it into responsive gameplay required significant research. Getting the feel right took several iterations. Too sensitive and the game felt uncontrollable, too stiff and it lost its appeal. Finding that balance was one of the most technically interesting problems I worked through during the week.

Alongside the core programming, I handled a significant part of the UI/UX design. This turned out to be its own design challenge. I am used to designing HUDs for desktop screens, where space is generous and the player uses a mouse. On mobile, every element competes for screen real estate, and placement must account for how the player physically holds the device. I had to rethink my instincts entirely, prioritising thumb-reachable zones and ensuring all key information was readable at a glance without obscuring the gameplay area.

This project gave me a strong foundation in mobile development and reinforced how much platform context shapes both technical and design decisions. Constraints, when embraced early, can become the most creative part of the process.
