Project summary

Snouff & Piktty is a mobile precision game created during a one-week game jam. The player must sort two types of creatures to the left or right while managing a global timer that changes according to successful and failed actions.

As Lead Programmer, I worked on the mobile input, game logic and a significant part of the UI/UX.

Design challenge

Our goal was to create an experience that felt genuinely designed for mobile, rather than moving a classic game mechanic onto a touchscreen. We therefore built the gameplay around the accelerometer.

This decision created a clear constraint: the player had to understand the action, control the device and read important information without the interface getting in the way of the play area.

Learning mobile input

I had never programmed for mobile before. I had to learn how to read accelerometer data, normalize it for devices with different sensitivities and translate it into sufficiently precise movement.

Finding the right balance required several iterations. Excessive sensitivity made the game difficult to control; a response that was too stiff removed the feeling of precision and responsiveness we were looking for.

Designing the mobile interface

I also handled a significant part of the UI/UX design. On desktop, the available space is more generous and the player uses a mouse. On mobile, every element must account for the screen size and the way the device is held.

UI/UX principles

Visible information
Understand the game state
Accessible areas
Respect the grip
Readable gameplay
Keep the action clear

I had to rethink my usual HUD design instincts: essential information needed to be readable quickly while keeping the play area clear.

What I learned

This project gave me a first concrete foundation in mobile development and showed me how strongly a platform’s context influences technical and design decisions.

The main lesson is that constraints must be considered from the beginning. The accelerometer, screen size and grip were not problems to solve at the end of the project: they directly defined the player experience.
